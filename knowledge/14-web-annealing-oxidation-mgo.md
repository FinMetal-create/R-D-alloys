## 1. Mechanism

### 1.1 Starting state of a rolled surface
- **Cold-rolled state:** a very thin, self-healing **amorphous Al2O3** film is present [W14-03] (abstract only). A typical
  "natural" oxide on aluminium is quoted as **2–10 nm** [W14-23] (snippet only). Another source cites 2–5 nm [W14-27] (snippet only).
- **Near-surface deformed layer:** shear deformation during rolling creates a surface layer that promotes **secondary
  intermetallic precipitation during subsequent heat treatment** [W14-19] (snippet only). Annealing rolled sheet **above 300 °C**
  creates a thin, electrochemically active **nanocrystalline surface layer** [W14-18] (snippet only; shown for AA8006). Hot rolling
  can also leave MgO-rich near-surface microstructures [W14-20] (snippet only). These layers are the industrial counterpart of
  a "Beilby layer". They are fine-grained and full of fast diffusion paths. W14-03 says the oxide thickens by **grain-boundary
  diffusion** during heat treatment (abstract only).

### 1.2 Mg surface/interface segregation
- AES from room temperature to 600 °C measured an **equilibrium Mg surface enrichment of about 24× at 100 °C and about 12× at 200 °C**.
  The surface was Mg-**depleted** at higher temperature in that experiment because Mg **evaporation** outpaced segregation
  [W14-04] (abstract only). This is a vacuum (AES) result. In air the segregated Mg is oxidised rather than evaporated (see 1.3).
- The thermodynamic model for ≤5 nm amorphous overgrowths found a **strong driving force for interfacial (chemical) segregation
  of Mg** in all cases studied [W14-08] (abstract only).
- In a molten Al-0.2 % Mg alloy (750 °C, dry air), the metal at the oxide interface contained **1.05 % Mg against 0.2 % bulk**
  [W14-11] (snippet only; liquid state).
- For alloys with **> 2.0 wt.% Mg**, the cold-rolled oxide was pure Al2O3. During medium-temperature brazing, Mg became strongly
  enriched at the outermost surface and formed an **MgO-based film** [W14-30] (snippet only).

### 1.3 Selective oxidation and the phase sequence
Thermodynamic background *(general knowledge)*: per mole of O2, MgO is more stable than Al2O3. Mg can therefore reduce an existing alumina
film (3 Mg + Al2O3 → 3 MgO + 2 Al) or combine with it to form spinel MgAl2O4. The sources below describe how this plays out.

## 2. Kinetics

### 2.1 Rate laws *(general knowledge forms)* and the regimes reported

- Thin-film (Cabrera–Mott-type) logarithmic or inverse-log growth: x = k_log·ln(1 + t/τ). This is typical of films up to a few nm at low T.
- Parabolic (diffusion-controlled): x² = k_p·t, with k_p = k_0·exp(−Q/RT).
- Linear (non-protective / breakaway): x = k_l·t. **Paralinear** = parabolic followed by linear.

| Regime reported | Conditions | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Near-limiting ~1 nm Al-oxide, then Mg-driven growth, films < 3 nm | 27–337 °C, low pO2 | [W14-09] (abstract only) |
| Parabolic → **linear** at long times | Al-3Mg, > 350 °C, air | [W14-05] (abstract only) |
| Parabolic for one alloy, **linear** for another at 450–500 °C | Al-Mg, 450–800 °C | [W14-15] (snippet only) |
| Growth **controlled by solid-state diffusion of Mg through the MgO–spinel layer** (0.4 % Mg). Mg diffusion plus **Mg-vapour transport across voids** at the substrate/oxide interface (higher Mg) | 550 °C, ≤ 90 h, dry air | [W14-01] (abstract only) |
| Paralinear; non-protective spinel | molten, 750 °C | [W14-11] (snippet only) |
| Amorphous film protective until it **crystallises**, then accelerated oxidation | molten Al-1…9 % Mg | [W14-32] (snippet only) |
| Two-stage: second stage caused by spinel formation once the shell exceeds ~1.5 µm. **Q ≈ 185 kJ/mol** for the spinel-controlled stage | Al-Mg **powders** in O2 | [W14-29] (snippet only) |

**Rate constants.** The abstracts retrieved do **not** give k_p values or activation energies for solid Al-Mg sheet at 300–450 °C.
The only Q found (185 kJ/mol) is for spinel-stage oxidation of powders [W14-29]. It is not transferable without checking.

### 2.3 Mg supply from the alloy: an order-of-magnitude bound (recalc.)

The inputs are *(general knowledge, verify)*: lattice diffusion of Mg in Al with D = D0·exp(−Q/RT), D0 = 1.24×10⁻⁴ m²/s and Q = 130.4 kJ/mol;
alloy density 2.70 g/cm³; MgO density 3.58 g/cm³ (60.3 wt.% Mg). The model assumes the surface is a perfect Mg sink (surface Mg = 0) on a
semi-infinite alloy. The Mg removed per unit area is then 2·c0·√(Dt/π). This amount is converted into an equivalent thickness of
**dense, pure MgO**. The result is an **upper bound** set by alloy-side supply. Real films contain Al2O3 and spinel, and oxide-side
transport may be slower.

| T (°C) | √(Dt), 1 h (µm) | MgO-equivalent, 1 h, 0.05 / 0.1 / 0.2 / 0.35 % Mg (nm) | 2 h, same Mg levels (nm) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 300 | 0.76 | 0.5 / 1.1 / 2.2 / 3.8 | 0.8 / 1.5 / 3.0 / 5.3 |
| 350 | 2.3 | 1.6 / 3.2 / 6.5 / 11.3 | 2.3 / 4.6 / 9.1 / 16.0 |
| 370 | 3.4 | 2.4 / 4.8 / 9.5 / 16.7 | 3.4 / 6.8 / 13.5 / 23.6 |
| 400 | 5.8 | 4.1 / 8.2 / 16.4 / 28.8 | 5.8 / 11.6 / 23.3 / 40.7 |
| 430 | 9.6 | 6.8 / 13.5 / 27.0 / 47.3 | 9.6 / 19.1 / 38.2 / 66.9 |
| 450 | 13.0 | 9.2 / 18.4 / 36.8 / 64.4 | 13.0 / 26.0 / 52.0 / 91.1 |

**Reading (Assessment).** For lean alloys, the Mg that bulk diffusion can deliver in a slug cycle corresponds to only a few to a few tens of
nm of MgO. This is the same order as the nm-scale oxide being tracked. Two consequences follow. (i) Bulk **Mg content enters
roughly linearly** whenever growth is supply-limited. The strong Mg depletion seen even at 0.05–0.1 % Mg [W14-06] and the
Mg-diffusion control reported at 0.4 % Mg [W14-01] are consistent with this. (ii) A slug with 0.3 % Mg can, in principle,
build roughly 3× the MgO of one with 0.1 % Mg under the same cycle. Near-surface deformed layers and grain boundaries
[W14-03], [W14-18] provide faster paths than the lattice D used here, so the bound may be exceeded locally.

## 3. Consequences

| Consequence | Evidence | Critical level reported? | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Discolouration (dark / "black" surface)** | Selective oxidation of Mg leaves **Al inclusions in the surface oxide**, which give a **black discolouration** (Al-3Mg, > 350 °C). The industrial patent targets discolouration of Al-Mg products during thermal treatment | none | [W14-05] (abstract only), [W14-16] (snippet only) |
| **Conversion-coating / pretreatment failure (→ lacquer adhesion)** | Excessive Mg surface enrichment (GDOES, TEM) **prevents formation of ZrO2-based conversion coatings**. The Mg and Zr surface concentrations are **anti-correlated**. **Sufficient alkaline etching** restores proper coating formation. Specimen variation tracked extrusion process details and **oxide thickness** more than bulk 6060 composition | none | [W14-21], [W14-22] (snippet only; the split of statements between the two papers is uncertain) |
| **Adhesive bonding** | The native heat-generated oxide is **inert and inhomogeneous** and must generally be **replaced** before bonding | none | [W14-24] (snippet only) |
| **Filiform corrosion (FFC) of painted sheet** | Annealing rolled sheet **> 300 °C** raises FFC susceptibility through an electrochemically active **nanocrystalline surface layer** [W14-18]. For AA3005-type alloys the **Mg-enriched oxide itself did not cause significant loss of FFC resistance**. The deformed surface layer with secondary precipitation is the main factor [W14-19], [W14-20]. A 2025 study on scrap-based Al links **Mg surface enrichment to increased FFC susceptibility** [W14-22] | none | (snippet only) — **sources disagree on whether MgO itself matters** |
| **Brazeability (CAB)** | MgO and spinel are **poorly soluble in molten flux**, and Mg reduces the surface Al2O3 [W14-23], [W14-25]. Patent background: **above ~0.3 % Mg the Nocolok flux is "poisoned"** by MgO formation (the Mg oxides react with the flux to magnesium fluorides), and raising the flux load is uneconomic [W14-26]. A flux supplier gives a tolerance of **0.5 % Mg** for furnace flux brazing [W14-25] | **Oxide 4 → 22 nm: only a small brazeability loss at 5 g/m² flux, but an appreciable loss of clad fluidity from 4 → 10 nm at 2 g/m² flux** [W14-25] | (snippet only) |
| **Lubricant (zinc stearate) interaction, friction in impact extrusion, weldability** | **No retrieved source** | — | — |

**Note.** The brazing figures are the only "critical thickness" values found. They show that a few nm of extra oxide can matter
when the reactive agent (flux) is scarce. Any analogy to lacquer, pretreatment or lubricant chemistry on slugs is an
*(inference)*, not evidence.

## 4. Mitigation and design levers

### 4.1 Alloy (Mg) limits
- Bulk Mg is the supply term (§2.3, recalc.). The literature contrast is 0.03 % Mg with limited MgO [W14-07] against 0.05–0.1 %
  Mg with strong Mg uptake at 577 °C [W14-06]. **Keeping Mg at the low end of the specification (and controlling Mg pick-up from
  scrap) is the most direct lever** *(inference from W14-06/W14-07 and §2.3)*.
- Reference points from neighbouring applications: ~0.3 % Mg (CAB flux poisoning, patent statements) [W14-26]; 0.5 % Mg (flux
  supplier tolerance) [W14-25]; ≥ 0.45 % Mg (lower bound of the discolouration patent's claimed range) [W14-16]. These are
  application-specific thresholds, **not** slug-quality limits.

### 4.2 Cycle design (temperature × time)
- The literature puts the change from Al-oxide-dominated to Mg-oxide-dominated surfaces at roughly **337–400 °C** for the
  conditions studied [W14-08], [W14-14]. Parabolic/linear growth is reported **above 350 °C** [W14-05], and Mg-rich surfaces
  over **350–600 °C** [W14-03]. **Lowering soak T below ~350 °C** should therefore shift the surface toward the thin-film regime
  *(inference)*. Higher cold reduction lowers the RX temperature (ch. 03, ch. 09 §3.3), which is what makes such a cycle possible.
- In a diffusion-controlled regime the oxide thickness scales with **√t**. Halving the time cuts thickness by only ~29 % at the same T
  *(recalc., general rate law)*. Temperature acts through k_p: for Q = 130–185 kJ/mol, 415 °C vs 400 °C gives **k_p × 1.7–2.1**,
  i.e. **thickness × 1.3–1.4** for equal time *(recalc.; Q values are general-knowledge Mg diffusion and [W14-29] powders)*.
- **Cooling** also contributes: oxidation continues above ~200 °C [W14-05], and Mg segregates even at 100–200 °C [W14-04].

### 4.3 Atmosphere
- **Inert gas (N2/Ar) only works if O2 and H2O are kept low.** In the brazing literature, 250–500 ppmv O2 already forms new oxide
  [W14-27], and Nocolok needs < 200 ppm O2 [W14-26] (snippet only). By extension, MgO is far more stable than Al2O3 (general knowledge),
  so a "nitrogen" anneal with a leaky retort or a wet charge may give little benefit *(inference)*.
- **Humidity/dew point** changes kinetics, morphology and composition [W14-03]. Water vapour can **initially slow** the oxidation of
  Al-4.2Mg [W14-02]. The sign of the dew-point effect for lean alloys at 300–450 °C is **not established** by what was retrieved.
- **CO2 additions** (0.5–5 %) are an industrially patented route against discolouration of Al-Mg products [W14-16]. The mechanism
  (an amorphous carbon cap over thin MgO) has been shown at 750 °C [W14-17]. The patent's alloy range starts at 0.45 % Mg.
  Applicability to 0.1–0.35 % Mg slugs at 300–450 °C is untested here.
- **Vacuum** is not neutral: Mg evaporates above 350 °C [W14-05], and surfaces become Mg-depleted under UHV heating [W14-04].

### 4.4 Surface condition and post-anneal treatment
- **Alkaline etching** removes the Mg-enriched surface and restores conversion-coating formation [W14-22]. **Acid pretreatment**
  dissolves Mg and Al oxides from Al-Mg sheet, leaving a surface Mg/Al atomic ratio of about 0.20 [W14-31] (snippet only).
  Bonding practice replaces the heat-generated oxide [W14-24].
- Removing or limiting the **near-surface deformed layer** (cleaning, etching before annealing) acts on the fast-diffusion
  and FFC-active layer [W14-18], [W14-19] *(inference for slugs)*. **Rolled-in lubricant residues** are a plausible factor but
  were not covered by any retrieved source.

### 4.5 Be micro-additions (historic)
- **Not verified in this round** (the search budget ran out before the query ran). *(general knowledge, verify)*: ppm-level Be has
  historically been used in Al-Mg melts to reduce Mg oxidation. Be and its compounds are toxic (inhalation hazard), and its use is now generally avoided for health and safety reasons. It should be treated as a historical reference, not a lever for aerosol slugs.

## 5. Assessment of the in-house MgO model (chapter 09 §3) — **Assessment, not source data**

**What chapter 09 documents.** The `mg_dashboard.xlsx` "Zayan diffusion model" gives cumulative MgO thickness per cycle. The
values run from 7.1 nm (A2 AO-45) to 12.1 nm (Cycle 3 Original). The regime labels are: "Al₂O₃ only" during the ramp to 300 °C,
"MgO LOG" for 300–370 °C, "Transition" at ~390–400 °C, "parabolic" above 400 °C, and "MgO nil <350 °C" on cooling. The risk
scoring weights time ≥ 400 °C highest (0.35). The model has no cited reference and no parameters. The evaluator inputs are
temperatures and times only (no Mg content, dew point or atmosphere). All scores assume no protective atmosphere.

**Consistent with the literature**
1. **A regime change at roughly 350–400 °C is supported.** The evidence is am-Al2O3 stability up to ~337 °C for ≤ 5 nm
   films [W14-08], an XPS Mg-oxide top layer above ~397 °C [W14-14], parabolic/linear growth above 350 °C [W14-05], and Mg-rich
   surfaces at 350–600 °C [W14-03]. The 400 °C "transition" boundary is therefore physically reasonable, but its exact value
   is not pinned down for 0.1–0.35 % Mg.
2. **Diffusion-controlled growth** is the mechanism Zayan et al. report for the 0.4 % Mg alloy [W14-01]. Choosing a
   diffusion (parabolic) law is thus traceable to the cited name.
3. **Shorter time and lower temperature reduce oxide.** The ranking direction (A2 AO-45 < A2 AO < … < Cycle 3) follows from any
   thermally activated growth law.
4. **The predicted magnitude (≈ 7–12 nm)** is of the same order as the alloy-side Mg-supply bound for 0.1–0.2 % Mg at 400–430 °C for 1–2 h
   (§2.3, recalc.), and it exceeds the natural oxide (2–10 nm, [W14-23]). The order of magnitude is plausible, but it is **not validated**.

**Divergences and gaps**
1. **Calibration range.** Zayan et al. studied **high-purity Al-0.4 and 2.0 % Mg at 550 °C for up to 90 h in dry air** [W14-01].
   Applying it to 0.1–0.35 % Mg (production purity), 370–455 °C and 1–3 h at temperature means extrapolating in Mg content,
   temperature and time all at once. Without the paper's rate constants (not retrieved), the model's k_p and Q cannot be
   traced to the source.
2. **No Mg-content term.** The literature (§1.4) and the supply bound (§2.3) both make bulk Mg a first-order variable. Under supply
   limitation, thickness is roughly ∝ c_Mg. The model should take Mg (and ideally Si and Fe, which affect the surface layer [W14-19]) as an input.
3. **"Al₂O₃ only" below 300 °C and "MgO nil < 350 °C" overstate the case.** Mg segregation is strong at 100–200 °C [W14-04], and
   Mg-driven oxide growth starts above ~112 °C in thin films [W14-09]. These sub-nm contributions are small, but the labels should read
   "slow", not "nil".
4. **Long-time kinetics may not stay parabolic.** A parabolic → linear (paralinear) change is reported for Al-3Mg above 350 °C
   [W14-05] and for some Al-Mg alloys at 450–500 °C [W14-15]. For lean alloys, Mg depletion should instead make growth
   **self-limiting** [W14-06]. Either way, a single parabolic law is a simplification.
5. **Atmosphere and humidity are absent.** Humidity controls the kinetics and composition [W14-03] and can slow the initial rate
   [W14-02]. CO2 suppresses discolouration [W14-16], [W14-17]. Residual O2 at the hundreds-of-ppm level still oxidises [W14-27].
   The model's note "inert gas reduces risk" is directionally right, but it needs an O2/H2O specification to mean anything.
6. **Surface state is absent.** Cold-rolled deformed layers, grain-boundary paths and the time since surface preparation all change
   the rate [W14-03], [W14-15], [W14-18]. The CR level therefore affects oxidation **directly** (through the surface layer), not only
   indirectly through shorter RX cycles.
7. **Phase identity.** The model reports "MgO". The literature shows MgO **and** spinel. Spinel tends to dominate with Mg depletion
   or longer exposure [W14-11], [W14-12], and the layering differs between studies [W14-01] vs [W14-06]. For coating/pretreatment
   response the relevant metric is probably **surface Mg enrichment** (e.g. GDOES Mg/Al ratio [W14-21], [W14-22]) rather than "MgO nm".
8. **Internal-consistency check (recalc.).** Suppose the time axis of the §3.2 growth curve matches the A2 AO-45 phase table in
   §3.5 (0–80 min ramp to 300 °C; 80–120 min 300→370 °C; equalise 370 °C to ~150 min; ~15 min to 400 °C; 50 min soak). Then
   the curve gives 0.10 → 3.41 nm during the 300→370 °C ramp and 5.96 nm by the end of equalisation. That is **≈ 84 % of the 7.12
   nm total formed at ≤ 370 °C**. The ramp to 400 °C plus the 50 min soak (~150–215 min) adds only ≈ 0.9 nm (5.96 → 6.82), and ≈ 0.3 nm forms on cooling. This contradicts the sheet's own
   labels ("MgO LOG", "LOG safe" at ≤ 370 °C) and its risk weighting (time ≥ 400 °C = "#1 MgO driver"). Also, Cycle 3 Original and the
   A1 test have no equalisation step (§3.1), yet they show the same 3.41 nm at 120 min as the A2 variants, which suggests a shared
   ramp template. **Either the time axis differs from the phase table, or the model's low-temperature growth term dominates.
   This should be checked against the cell formulas.**
9. **Resolution vs. uncertainty.** The best cycles (7.1 vs 8.6 vs 9.1 nm) differ by roughly 5–30 %. Without measured
   thickness data, and given the regime and atmosphere effects above, these differences are probably below what the model can
   resolve. The ranking by time-at-temperature is more robust than the absolute nm values.

**Suggested validation (Assessment).** (a) Measure the surface Mg and O depth profile with **GDOES** (fast, works on production
slugs) and/or **XPS/AES sputter profiles** (as in [W14-03], [W14-07]). Sample slugs from at least three real cycles (e.g. Cycle 1,
Cycle 3, A1) plus as-rolled reference slugs, at two positions in the load. (b) Record the furnace dew point and O2 level and the
bulk Mg of each lot. (c) Fit x² = k_p(T)·t or a supply-limited form with c_Mg as an input. (d) Correlate the result with the
actual quality defect (discolouration, lacquer adhesion, pretreatment response, extrusion friction). This last point is still
open question 8 of chapter 09.
