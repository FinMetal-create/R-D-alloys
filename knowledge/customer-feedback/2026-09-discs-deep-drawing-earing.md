# Customer feedback: 3.4 mm deep-drawing discs, AlMn1 recycled alloy (Sept 2026)

**Product:** discs Ø280–350 mm × 3.4 mm, deep drawn into frying pans (Ø22–28) and pots (Ø18–20); some discs are
coated before forming, some after.
**Alloy delivered:** the recycled AlMn1 alloy (Mn 1.0–1.5, Fe ≤ 0.9, Cu ≤ 0.2, Mg ≤ 0.1 datasheet; ≥ 98 % scrap).
**Sources:** customer presentation "TALUM SEPT 26" (8 pages, 2026-09-16) and the in-house note
"Tefal_redukcije_kv.171" (rolling schedules of two casts). Both shared by the user on 2026-10-14. Values are
reproduced as received. The analysis in §4 is inference from the cited chapters.

## 4. Assessment (inference)

1. **The 45° ears and pancake grains have the signature of Mn precipitating during the final anneal.**
   - At 1.0–1.1 % Mn with no homogenisation, the rotary-cast and quenched strip carries a large Mn
     supersaturation [ch19 §1.3].
   - During a slow (batch) heat-up, dispersoids precipitate on the deformed structure before RX. The result is
     coarse, elongated grains and textures that keep rolling-type and P components [ch03 §5.2; ch15 W15-28/W15-29].
   - Precipitation during annealing "weakens cube and strengthens R" [ch03 §8.2, HBAL03 p.235].
   - Rolling-type components (Brass, Copper, S) give **four ears at 45°**; cube gives 0/90° [ch03 §8.3, HH Tab.15.2].
   - Mechanical fibring from elongated grains adds to the anisotropy [ch03 §8.4, Pol06 p.40].
2. **The low-Rp0.2 discs are the bad ones.**
   - Rp0.2 of 34–36 MPa goes with "grain not OK" and 8 % earing; 42–49 MPa with 4–5.5 %. This fits coarser grains
     (Hall–Petch, R16) and possibly less Mn left in solution.
   - The discs share one chemistry, so the scatter points to **coil-to-coil or load-position differences in heating
     rate** during a batch anneal. Heating rate decides whether precipitation or RX wins [ch03 §5.2] (to be
     confirmed with load records).
3. **The surface-to-centre heterogeneity matches the rotary-cast solidification gradient.**
   - The chilled surface has finer constituents and higher supersaturation. Its particles are mostly below the PSN
     critical size (≈ 3.3–4.5 µm at 40–50 % cold reduction [ch03 §3]), so it recrystallises with few nuclei and
     coarser grains.
   - The slower-cooled centre has coarser constituents (more PSN), so its grains are finer.
   - Coarse surface grains cause **orange peel** [ch05 §4.11, K p.210].
   - High-temperature homogenisation of twin-roll-cast 3105 reduced the surface-to-centre particle gradient, and
     cyclic homogenisation up to 620 °C gave a uniform fine grain structure through the thickness [ch15, W15-11].
4. **The cold reduction is low.**
   - 43 % (OT3), and possibly only 24 % after an intermediate anneal (OT1).
   - The in-house CRM tables ask for ε ≥ 0.85 (≥ 57 %) for 3207 and ε ≥ 0.95 (≥ 61 %) for 3003 [ch09 §5].
   - Low cold strain gives few nuclei, coarse grains and more scatter.
5. **Why other suppliers don't have the problem (inference).** With the customer chemistry (Mn 0.50–0.60), Mn
   supersaturation is roughly halved. Typical suppliers also use DC ingot with homogenisation or a fast-heating
   anneal. Both remove the precipitation-versus-RX race.

| 1 | **Make the customer chemistry**: Mn 0.50–0.60, Fe 0.35–0.40, Si 0.15–0.20, Cu ≤ 0.04, Ti 0.020–0.030 (a tight 3207). Record the load-centre and outer-wrap thermocouples | RX before precipitation [ch03 §5.2; W15-28] | Fine equiaxed grains, weak R/P components |

| 5 | **Per-coil release tests**: EN 1669 cup test (earing % and ear position), grain size at surface and centre (Barker), Rp0.2 with a coarse-grain flag below ≈ 40 MPa (from §1, provisional), chemistry against the customer window | – | The guarantees the customer asked for |
| 6 | **Surface defects** (scratches, black stains): separate investigation, starting with SEM-EDS of the stains, rolling-oil residue and burn-off in the anneal, water carry-over from the cooling bath, interleaving and packaging (general knowledge; not yet in the knowledge base) | – | – |
