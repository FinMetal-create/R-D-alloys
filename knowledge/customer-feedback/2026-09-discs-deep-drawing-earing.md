coated before forming, some after.

## 4. Assessment (inference)

1. **The 45° ears and pancake grains have the signature of Mn precipitating during the final anneal.**
   - At 1.0–1.1 % Mn with no homogenisation, the rotary-cast and quenched strip carries a large Mn
     supersaturation [ch19 §1.3].
   - During a slow (batch) heat-up, dispersoids precipitate on the deformed structure before RX. The result is
     coarse, elongated grains and textures that keep rolling-type and P components [ch03 §5.2; ch15 W15-28/W15-29].
   - Precipitation during annealing "weakens cube and strengthens R" [ch03 §8.2, HBAL03 p.235].
   - Rolling-type components (Brass, Copper, S) give **four ears at 45°**; cube gives 0/90° [ch03 §8.3, HH Tab.15.2].
   - Mechanical fibring from elongated grains adds to the anisotropy [ch03 §8.4, Pol06 p.40].
2. **The low-Rp0.2 discs are the bad ones.**
   - Rp0.2 of 34–36 MPa goes with "grain not OK" and 8 % earing; 42–49 MPa with 4–5.5 %. This fits coarser grains
     (Hall–Petch, R16) and possibly less Mn left in solution.
   - The discs share one chemistry, so the scatter points to **coil-to-coil or load-position differences in heating
     rate** during a batch anneal. Heating rate decides whether precipitation or RX wins [ch03 §5.2] (to be
     confirmed with load records).
3. **The surface-to-centre heterogeneity matches the rotary-cast solidification gradient.**
   - The chilled surface has finer constituents and higher supersaturation. Its particles are mostly below the PSN
     critical size (≈ 3.3–4.5 µm at 40–50 % cold reduction [ch03 §3]), so it recrystallises with few nuclei and
     coarser grains.
   - The slower-cooled centre has coarser constituents (more PSN), so its grains are finer.
   - Coarse surface grains cause **orange peel** [ch05 §4.11, K p.210].
   - High-temperature homogenisation of twin-roll-cast 3105 reduced the surface-to-centre particle gradient, and
     cyclic homogenisation up to 620 °C gave a uniform fine grain structure through the thickness [ch15, W15-11].
4. **The cold reduction is low.**
   - 43 % (OT3, ε 0.57) and 51 % (OT1, ε 0.72; no intermediate anneal, user 2026-10-14).
   - The in-house CRM tables ask for ε ≥ 0.85 (≥ 57 %) for 3207 and ε ≥ 0.95 (≥ 61 %) for 3003 [ch09 §5].
   - Low cold strain gives few nuclei, coarse grains and more scatter.
5. **Cold reduction alone does not explain the scatter.** The three 1st-delivery discs probably come from one
   route, yet earing ranges from 4.2 % to 8.4 %. If the 1st delivery is cast 21592 (51 %) and the 2nd is cast
   23321 (43 %), the higher reduction did *not* give lower earing (8.1–8.4 % vs 5.3–5.5 %). Either way, most of
   the variation must come from the final anneal (heating rate by coil and load position) and from Mn in
   solution, not from the reduction. The delivery-to-cast mapping is still to be confirmed.
6. **Why other suppliers don't have the problem (inference).** With the customer chemistry (Mn 0.50–0.60), Mn
   supersaturation is roughly halved. Typical suppliers also use DC ingot with homogenisation or a fast-heating
   anneal. Both remove the precipitation-versus-RX race.

## 6. Two-step final anneal for AlMn1 discs (if the alloy is kept)

*Recipe and trial proposal (inference). The only literature data point is one twin-roll-cast 3003 study at
abstract level [ch15, W15-35].*

**Evidence.** On cold-rolled TRC 3003, 450 °C / 5 h ("pre-recovery") + 530 °C / 15 h gave fine quasi-equiaxed
grains, a very weak ND-rotated cube and **Δr = 0.03, against 0.31** for a single 530 °C / 15 h step [W15-35].
Δr = (r₀ − 2r₄₅ + r₉₀)/2 is the earing index: the higher Δr, the more earing [ch05 §4.11, TOT p.851–853].

**Mechanism** [ch15 §3, W15-35/W15-36; ch03 §5.2]:
1. **Step 1 (≈ 450 °C, below the RX start of this supersaturated, moderately rolled strip).** Mn precipitates
   while the structure only recovers. Precipitate-free zones form around the coarse constituents, so the
   deformation zones there are free of fine dispersoids.
2. **Step 2 (≈ 530 °C).** The dispersoids coarsen, so Zener drag falls (P_Z ∝ F_V/r, R12). RX then starts by PSN
   in the precipitate-free zones. PSN nuclei are randomly oriented, which weakens the texture.
3. **Why it suits a batch furnace.** The Mn precipitation is finished, everywhere in the load, before RX starts.
   The heating rate of each coil or load position therefore no longer decides the result. That addresses the
   coil-to-coil scatter in §1 (4.2–8.4 % earing).

**Do not reverse the order.** In CC AA3015, high-then-low (599 → 371 °C) gave faster RX but **coarser** grains and
a **stronger** cube [W15-30].

**Limits:**
- It does not fix the surface-to-centre constituent-size gradient of the rotary-cast strip. That needs
  hot-band homogenisation (ch19 P1, [W15-11]).
- Its effect may be smaller at 43–51 % cold reduction than in the thin TRC sheet of [W15-35], because fewer
  particles are large enough for PSN. Combine it with ≈ 60 % cold reduction (§5 action 2).
- The cycle is long (≈ 20 h at temperature plus heat-up). Keep good oil burn-off below ≈ 300 °C so the long cycle
  does not bake in stains (general knowledge; see §1 black stains).

**Lab trial** (as-rolled 3.4 mm strip from production, taken before the final anneal):

| A | current plant cycle | – | Baseline |

- **Check after step 1** (extra samples pulled out):
  - not recrystallised (Barker, hardness still above the O temper);
  - conductivity up, meaning Mn has precipitated.
- **After the full cycle:**
  - earing (customer's cup test / EN 1669) and r₀, r₄₅, r₉₀ → Δr;
  - grain size at the surface and the centre;
  - Rp0.2, Rm, A50.
- **Plant transfer.** Run the best cycle on one coil, with thermocouples at the coil core and outer wrap. Count
  the step-1 hold from the moment the core reaches temperature.
